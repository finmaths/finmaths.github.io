Earn itWhich job pays the most?

1Ways of earning money

  • Wage = hourly rate × hours. Salary = a set amount per year
  • Commission and royalties are a % of sales. Piecework is pay per item
  • ICT diagnostic quiz checks %, decimals, time and tables
  • 5% written as 0.5. Commission worked out on the retainer

2Overtime and penalty rates

  • Pay = base rate × multiplier × hours
  • Time and a half × 1.5, double time × 2
  • Overtime rate changes after 2 hours (real Fair Work pay guide)
  • Adding 1.5 instead of multiplying. Adding the hours

3Gross to take-home pay

  • 1 year = 52 weeks, so a month is 52 ÷ 12 ≈ 4.33 weeks
  • Leave loading: 17.5% of 4 weeks' pay. Casuals get no paid leave
  • Net pay = gross pay − tax withheld (ATO tax table)
  • Monthly pay = 4 × weekly pay. Adding the tax

3bTaxable income (outline)

  • Income − allowable deductions, 2026–27 resident tax rates

Spend or borrow itWhat does it really cost?

7Paying over time

  • Total paid = deposit + all repayments
  • Extra cost = total paid − cash price
  • A smaller monthly payment means it is cheaper

8Buy now, pay later

  • Payment schedule includes the payment made today
  • Account charges and late fees, not interest
  • ICT spreadsheet model checked against hand working
  • 'Interest-free' means it costs nothing extra

9Short-term loans

  • Total repaid = principal + fees
  • Borrowing cost = total repaid − principal
  • ICT duration model. AoL spending assessment
  • Fee worked out on the wrong amount

How the unit flows

The unit follows money in the order students meet it. They earn it (Lessons 1–3), let it grow (Lessons 4–6), then spend or borrow it (Lessons 7–9). Each part has one driving question, and its lessons build towards answering it.

Part Driving question Progression
Earning money (Lessons 1–3) Which job pays the most? Name the ways of being paid → calculate extra pay → compare jobs over the same period and find take-home pay
Simple interest (Lessons 4–6) How does money grow over time? To be added
Spending money (Lessons 7–9) What does it really cost? Deposit and repayments → extra charges and conditions → comparing borrowing costs
