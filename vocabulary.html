Unit vocabulary

Every term taught in the unit, grouped by part. Each lesson ends with its own list, with the photos and diagrams that go with the terms.

Earning money (Lessons 1–3)

TermMeaningTaught in
incomeMoney a person receives, for example from workingLesson 1
wagePay worked out as hours worked × hourly rateLesson 1, Lesson 2
hourly rateThe amount paid for each hour of workLesson 1, Lesson 2
salaryA set amount of pay for a year, paid in equal parts (for example weekly or monthly)Lesson 1
per annum (p.a.)Per yearLesson 1, Lesson 3
commissionPay that is a percentage of the value of what a person sellsLesson 1
retainerA set amount paid each week on top of commission, whatever the salesLesson 1
pieceworkPay for each item produced or each task completedLesson 1
royaltyA payment to the creator of a book, song or other work each time it is sold or usedLesson 1
shiftOne block of hours worked in a dayLesson 2
overtimeHours worked beyond a worker's normal hours, usually paid at a higher rateLesson 2
penalty rateA higher rate paid for working at times such as weekends, public holidays or late at nightLesson 2
multiplierThe number the base rate is multiplied by, for example × 1.5 or × 2Lesson 2
time and a halfBase rate × 1.5Lesson 2
double timeBase rate × 2Lesson 2
public holidayAn official holiday, for example Christmas Day, when penalty rates usually applyLesson 2
pay guideA Fair Work Ombudsman summary of the minimum pay rates in an awardLesson 2
gross payPay before any tax is taken outLesson 3
net pay (take-home pay)Pay left after tax and other deductions are taken outLesson 3
fortnightTwo weeksLesson 3
PAYG withholdingPay As You Go: the employer takes some tax out of each pay and sends it to the ATOLesson 3
tax withheldThe amount of tax taken out of one payLesson 3
tax-free thresholdFor most Australian residents, the first $18,200 of yearly income, on which no income tax is paid. Claiming it means less tax is taken out of each payLesson 3
annual leavePaid time off work. Full-time and part-time employees get 4 weeks a year, but casual employees do notLesson 3
leave loadingAn extra payment during annual leave, worked out as a percentage of normal pay (17.5% in the General Retail Industry Award)Lesson 3

Simple interest (Lessons 4–6)

TermMeaningTaught in
principal (P)The original amount invested. For Ella, P = $1000.Lesson 4
simple interest (I)Interest calculated on the original principal for the whole stated period.Lesson 4
interest rate (r)Interest per period as a proportion of the principal. 5% p.a. is r = 0.05 per year.Lesson 4
per annum (p.a.)Per year. Match n to years when the rate is annual.Lesson 4
time (n)The number of periods for the quoted rate. With an annual rate, 18 months is n = 1.5 years.Lesson 4
total amount (A)The principal plus accumulated interest: A = P + I.Lesson 4
unknown quantityA value not yet given, such as r or n.Lesson 5
substituteReplace a symbol with its known value, using compatible units.Lesson 5
inverse operationAn operation that undoes another. Division undoes multiplication.Lesson 5
verifySubstitute the solution into the original rule to check it gives the stated interest.Lesson 5
coordinate (n, A)An ordered pair giving time first and total amount second.Lesson 5
linear relationshipA relationship with a constant change in amount for equal changes in time. Its graph is a straight line.Lesson 5
initial valueThe amount when n = 0. For an amount graph, it is the principal P.Lesson 6
gradient / rate of changeChange in amount divided by change in time. With n in years, the unit is dollars per year.Lesson 6
linearThe amount changes by equal increments for equal time intervals, so the graph is a straight line.Lesson 6
coefficient of nThe number multiplying n. In A = 2000 + 120n, 120 is the yearly dollar increase Pr.Lesson 6
representationA way to show a relationship, such as a rule, table or graph.Lesson 6
annual percentage rateThe yearly interest as a percentage of the principal. It differs from the dollar increase Pr.Lesson 6

Spending money (Lessons 7–9)

TermMeaningTaught in
cash pricePrice paid for the item outright.Lesson 7
depositInitial amount paid before the later repayments.Lesson 7
instalment / repaymentOne scheduled payment.Lesson 7
durationHow long the payment plan lasts.Lesson 7
repayment totalSum of the scheduled repayments, excluding the initial deposit in these offers.Lesson 7
total paidDeposit plus all repayments and any separately stated charges.Lesson 7
additional costTotal paid minus cash price.Lesson 7
affordabilityWhether a person can meet payments while covering other needs and commitments.Lesson 7
BNPLA payment arrangement that divides a purchase into scheduled payments.Lesson 8
interest-freeNo interest is charged under the stated conditions; other charges may still apply.Lesson 8
account chargeA separately stated charge for the payment arrangement.Lesson 8
late feeAn additional charge when a specified payment is late.Lesson 8
payment scheduleThe dates and amounts of required payments.Lesson 8
conditional totalTotal for a stated scenario, such as exactly one late payment.Lesson 8
principalOriginal amount borrowed in these offers.Lesson 9
establishment feeOne-off charge for setting up the loan.Lesson 9
fee basisThe amount on which a percentage fee is calculated.Lesson 9
loan termTime over which the loan is repaid.Lesson 9
total repaidAll payments returned to the lender, including stated charges.Lesson 9
borrowing costTotal repaid minus the amount borrowed.Lesson 9
repayment ceilingA stated maximum payment; it does not by itself establish affordability.Lesson 9
